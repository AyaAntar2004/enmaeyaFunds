What this view suggests

What is this?

1

This dashboard tracks development funding in Lebanon — showing where money is allocated across government ministries, sectors, implementing organizations, and geographic locations. It's built to make this information accessible to the public, promoting transparency and helping users understand the country's development landscape.

Why it matters

2

Development funding shapes which sectors expand, where jobs are created, and where innovation opportunities emerge.

  • Students can see which fields have growing demand
  • Job seekers can spot expanding sectors and active employers
  • Startups can find underserved sectors and procurement openings
  • NGOs and researchers can track donors, gaps, and partnership openings

Mission & Vision

3

Mission: make development funding in Lebanon transparent, accessible, and understandable — giving the public what it needs to see national priorities, emerging sectors, and the opportunities they create for education, employment, entrepreneurship, and research.

Vision: a Lebanon where development information is open and accessible to everyone, enabling more informed decisions by citizens, institutions, businesses, and development partners.

Who this data helps

Practical takeaways for people using this dashboard.

For Students

Development, health, and social-sector fields show the most active project pipelines right now.

Programs in public health, environmental policy, and social protection align closely with current funding priorities.

For Job Seekers

Health and Development show the highest project volume, suggesting steadier hiring demand in those sectors.

Beirut and Mount Lebanon concentrate the most project activity, and likely the most roles.

For Startups

AI & Technology is the smallest funded sector — a potential underserved niche for new ventures.

66 projects are recorded nationally rather than regionally, suggesting demand for solutions that scale beyond one area.

For NGOs

World Bank, EU, and UNICEF are the most active funders to approach for partnership conversations.

MoSA's broad partner network (9 organizations) suggests openness to new implementing partners.

For Researchers

AI & Technology and Education are comparatively data-poor and underfunded relative to their long-run importance.

Funder concentration (e.g. MoPWT's 57% single-funder dependency) is a notable risk area worth investigating.
